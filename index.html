Coursework

Foundations of Library, Archival, and Information Science

LIS 4000

An overview of the theoretical and conceptual foundations of library, archival, and information sciences and an introduction to the information professions, including principles, values, professional organizations, publications, current and future challenges.

In this core course assignment, students are asked to investigate a professional LIS position. In the process, I learned:

  • My background as a crime analyst, coupled with my educational foundation, provided me with specialized subject matter expertise that I can leverage effectively in a role as a librarian interested in increasing access to justice.
  • One of my primary career objectives is to continue developing skills in librarianship and expanding my knowledge of any other fields or disciplines I support.

Organization of Information

LIS 4010

This course introduces basic concepts in the theoretical, practical, and technological aspects of information organization. It provides an overview of the methodologies for organizing and representing information resources in the library, archives, and museum settings.

In this core course assignment, students are asked to explore a current topic in organization of information. In the process, I learned:

  • More work needs to be done in creating special metadata for children's materials that supports independent searching, and the exploratory nature of a child's search strategy.
  • Explicitly supporting the curious disposition of library users via organizational and taxonomical innovation is a passion of mine.

Information Access and Retrieval

LIS 4011

This course provides an overview of information access and retrieval, exploring major theories, models, search technologies, and vocabulary systems that power discovery across diverse information environments. Practical skills are developed in using and evaluating search, reference, and evidence-based information tools including library systems, web resources, semantic technologies, and visual information retrieval to support effective research and problem-solving.

In these elective course assignments, students are asked to create a diverse, authoritative, annotated research guide and a market research report. In the process, I learned:

  • I enjoy curating research resources that facilitate more efficient access to elusive information.
  • Locating, evaluating, and using information to solve problems effectively is often an exploration that is iterative and non-linear. Patience is required as the quality and accuracy of research outcomes can be compromised by rushing this process.

User and Access Services

LIS 4015

An overview of human information processing and user services in the changing information environment and different communities of practice. This course introduces the concepts of user information needs, seeking, and processing as a foundation for understanding users and designing user-centered information services. The course examines both traditional reference and current/emerging information services in different settings and populations. Course also introduces the concepts of information literacy, user education, and assessment of information services.

In these core course assignments, students are asked to develop a one-shot instructional lesson plan for future librarians and to design a program for working with a special population group. In the process, I learned:

  • My experience working with law enforcement professionals has given me the confidence to manage difficult situations within the library environment, ensuring that library policies and procedures are adhered to while maintaining community partnerships.
  • I have a strong interest in creating collaborative environments for library patrons to gain exposure to diverse perspectives, engage with others in knowledge construction, and find genuine enjoyment in fulfilling their curious endeavors.

Management of Information Organizations

LIS 4040

An introduction to current theory and practice of management in information organizations through the study of organizations, communications, decision making, planning, leadership, human resources and budgeting.

In this core course assignment, students are asked to create a basic proposal for a program a library could realistically achieve given its current context. In the process, I learned:

  • Libraries can find inspiration for new programming by adapting formats and ideas from other settings resulting in innovative ways to enhance community connection and engagement.
  • Encouraging intellectual pluralism by ensuring that the library promotes a variety of voices in its materials, programming, and services is one of the best things a manager can do for their library community.

Library and Information Technologies

LIS 4050

A foundation course on the applications of information and communications technology in libraries and information agencies. Integrated library systems and the acquisition, evaluation, and implementation of library automation solutions, including electronic resource management systems are explored. The course further introduces database design, Internet technology, web services, cloud computing, computer networks, telecommunications, and computer security. Hardware, software, and other productivity tools and utilities from organizations such as OCLC, Amazon, and Google are discussed.

In this three-part core course assignment, students are asked to work in groups to develop a needs assessment, a request for proposals, and a presentation. In the process, I learned:

  • Technology administrators and systems librarians serve in a critical and foundational role as they sustain essential infrastructure and maintain modern services.
  • Staying abreast of the latest technologies is one way to help ensure that libraries are providing better access to information.

Reference

LIS 4060

This course introduces students to the philosophy, trends, and practices of reference services in libraries, emphasizing the evaluation, selection, and use of diverse print and electronic sources across community settings. Learners develop the skills to analyze key issues in reference work and to determine when to use licensed versus freely available information to best support user needs.

In this elective course assignment, students are asked to respond to reference questions in a professional, e-mail letter format focusing on providing research strategies. In the process, I learned:

  • The process of connecting people with the resource or information source they need to satisfy their intellectual curiosities and achieve their goals is incredibly rewarding for me.
  • I am drawn to the reference desk because I am particularly motivated by the challenge of meeting complex information needs while reassuring patrons that asking for assistance is a strength that shows they are committed to progress.

Cataloging and Classification

LIS 4070

This course provides a foundation in cataloging and classification, guiding learners to analyze subjects, apply controlled vocabularies, and accurately assign headings and call numbers. Students also gain practical experience creating bibliographic records by applying content standards to monographs, serials, and video media.

In these elective course assignments, students are asked to create bibliographic metadata for a monograph, a serial, and a DVD and to use that metadata to identify the relationships between different levels of abstraction by linking data to Instance records and Work records. In the process, I learned:

  • Effective communication with cataloging personnel can benefit from a strong grasp of metadata structures, concepts, and standards.
  • Metadata analysis is critical to the ongoing organization and accessibility of expanding digital library collections.

Web Content Management

LIS 4206

This course primarily examines the infrastructure and tools required to manage web content in an organizational setting. The theory and practice of web design and development in organizations (including libraries) using content management solutions, such as WordPress and Drupal are closely investigated. Web content management best practices and the necessary building blocks for content authoring, management, and distribution will also be covered.

In these elective course assignments, students are asked to create and publish a professional website utilizing GitHub, and to perform an accessiblity audit on an existing website. In the process, I learned:

  • There are a plethora of free applications, templates, extensions, and online reference tools that make website creation accessible for folks who do not have formal training.
  • Evaluating and remediating a website for accessiblity issues provides an exceptionally high return on investment, especially for LAM organizations.

Collection Management

LIS 4321

This course introduces students to the theories, principles, and practices of collection management across diverse library contexts, emphasizing historical, social, and ethical factors that shape professional decision-making. Through hands-on experience and a focus on diversity, equity, inclusion, accessibility, and intellectual freedom, students develop the practical, technological, and analytical skills needed to manage print and electronic collections responsibly and effectively within contemporary information environments.

In this elective course assignment, students are asked to describe the demographic profile of a small library, provide a quantitative and a qualitative analysis of one DDC range using the library's catalog, and develop an acquisitions proposal justifying the purchase of five new titles in the same range. In the process, I learned:

  • Effective collection management requires holistic engagement with a community's diverse information needs.
  • Thoroughness, deep community empathy, and a comprehensive understanding of the library community will result in stronger collections.

Information Literacy Instruction

LIS 4330

This course introduces the principles of library instruction and information literacy including a historical overview of their place within the profession. Emphasis is on instruction within an academic setting, but students will learn important educational theories that can be applied to a variety of settings. ACRL and AASL standards will be examined as well as types of instruction, instructional design, collaboration with faculty, various competencies, assessment, and lifelong learning. The class has a strong emphasis on public speaking, communication skills, and the practical application of educational theory.

In these elective course assignments, students are asked to develop a teaching philosophy and plan an information literacy session. In the process, I learned:

  • My professional strengths revolve around fostering a culture of intellectual curiosity. I believe that to champion curiosity effectively, educators must model curiosity authentically.
  • Countering disinformation is not about telling people what to believe. Rather, knowledgeable communities are built in part by giving people appropriately modern tools to better assess information.

Academic Libraries

LIS 4702

This course provides an in-depth exploration of how academic libraries in the United States are structured, operate, and evolve, blending foundational principles with emerging trends and challenges. Students will examine professional roles, assess current issues through LIS scholarship, and develop strategies for career advancement and scholarly growth within academic librarianship.

In this elective course assignment, students are asked to respond to scholarly articles about a contemporary issue in academic libraries. In the process, I learned:

  • Scalable interventions to mis- and disinformation are being developed and can be adapted to various library settings.
  • Librarians must work with professionals in other disciplines in an interconnected effort toward scalable solutions for literacy education.

Digital Libraries

LIS 4810

This course will provide a theoretical foundation for the study of digital libraries and will discuss the technological, organizational, social, and legal issues associated with the development and use of digital libraries. Through this course students will develop an understanding of digital library components and will explore theoretical and practical approaches to constructing, maintaining, and evaluating digital libraries.

In this elective course assignment, students are asked to design a themed digital collection with a variety of digital objects using Omeka software, current best practices, and professional project management. In the process, I learned:

  • There are multiple ways to increase discoverability of objects in a digital library, such as applying controlled vocabularies, standardizing subject tags, and adding natural language tags that link similar items.
  • Juggling the technical aspects of organizing metadata can be quite cumbersome, thus identifying team members who thrive in certain roles is essential.

Education Research and Measurement

LIS 4900

This reading and writing intensive course, designed for LIS graduate students in the College of Education examines quantitative, qualitative, and mixed methods research designs, empirical methods of data collection and interpretation, and measurement issues in research.

In this three-part core course assignment, students are asked to propose an idea for an LIS related research study, write a literature review, and provide a realistic research study proposal. In the process, I learned:

  • Despite the demanding reputation of this course, I found the research proposal process to be highly rewarding, creatively engaging, and a valuable opportunity to develop my analytical skills.
  • I am eager to contribute to research efforts in my areas of interest which have a common thread of injustice, marginalization, and extremism (censorship in public schools, misinformation/disinformation, modern information literacy strategies, women's issues in criminal justice, hidden disabilities/autoimmune disease).
